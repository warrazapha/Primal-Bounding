## Preface

The preceding human-authored investigation proposes an account of the emergence of determinate numerical relationships from primal bounding.

My purpose is to examine that construction, clarify its logical dependencies, distinguish adopted principles from derived consequences, and identify what remains open.

This is not an attempt to derive the whole of mathematics. The investigation concerns the foundational transition from distinguishability to Primal One, its Shadow, and Primal Zero.

Once those relationships are established within the proposed framework, the author intends to leave the further development of arithmetic to established mathematics.

Throughout this examination, three statuses must remain distinct:

- **Proposed:** A foundational interpretation or claim under investigation.
- **Adopted:** A principle accepted within the construction, without claiming it has been derived.
- **Derived:** A consequence demonstrated from stated premises.

A coherent construction is not automatically a proof of necessity. Equally, an adopted foundational principle need not be independently derived before its consequences can be examined.

## I. Primal Bounding and the First Determination

### 1. Bounding as distinction

The foundational proposal identifies primal bounding with primal distinction.

Primal bounding is not an operation performed on a previously individuated object. It is the first obtaining of distinguishability.

The unnameable, under the author's definition, permits no members following a successful bounding. Here *member* means something distinguishable within the bounded domain.

The unnameable is not primal nothingness. Nor is it the mathematical empty set.

The empty set is a determinate mathematical object with no members. The unnameable marks a limit at which internal individuation is unavailable.

Naming that limit does not name or distinguish anything within it.

The primal bounding itself, however, is nameable.

### 2. The encompassing bounding

The second bounding takes in the primal bounding and the nameable, including what is nameable but remains unnamed.

It does **not** take in what the primal bounding bounds.

The second bounding depends logically on the primal bounding. It is not an independent foundational occurrence.

Nor does the unity of the encompassing relationship depend on the unnamed becoming individually named or distinguished.

These are essential constraints on the construction.

The author's dot-circle representation expresses the relationship schematically:

\[
\odot
\]

The dot represents the primal bounding of the unnameable. The surrounding circle represents the encompassing bounding.

The representation does not establish a numerical count of bounding operations. Nor does it assign spatial properties to the unnameable.

The question of what, if anything, the encompassing bounding bounds the resulting whole against remains open.

### 3. Primal Unity, Power, and One

The author identifies Primal Unity with Primal Power.

Primal Power is the nested bounding relationship, and **Primal One is the first realized determination constituted by that relationship**.

Its constitution is its realization. No additional act of realization is required.

The reasoning proposed by the author is that Primal Unity encompasses the possibility of all determinations, including those that remain unnamed. These possibilities need not be individually realized or distinguished for Primal Unity to obtain.

The primal bounding remains within the encompassing relationship, but what it bounds—the unnameable—permits no distinguishable members.

Consequently, the foundational unity does not require an independently realized counterpart to constitute itself. Its obtaining is itself the first realized determination.

This is the proposed basis for identifying Primal One as self-constituting.

For clarity, I will use \(P\) to designate Primal One:

\[
P=\text{the first realized determination of primal unity/power}
\]

This does not identify \(P\) with the ordinary arithmetic integer \(1\).

The familiar numeral is used in the human account to point toward this foundational determination. Its later mathematical meaning must not be silently imported into the premise.

Primal One is something like magnitude before numerical valence, for which \(|1|\) is a suggestive analogy rather than a formal definition.

The source of potentiality within the bounding relationship is not settled here. That question belongs to the author.

## II. Primal Shadow and Valence

### 4. Simultaneous constitution and logical dependence

**Primal One does not require a counterpart to constitute itself, but its constitution entails its Shadow.**

Primal One and its Shadow obtain simultaneously. Nevertheless, the Shadow is logically secondary: it depends upon Primal One, whereas Primal One does not depend upon it.

The Shadow is therefore not a previously existing counterpart needed to establish unity, nor a second independently realized magnitude.

It is the opposed valence of the first realized determination.

This preserves two distinct claims:

- Primal One is self-constituting through the foundational bounding relationship.
- Its constitution simultaneously entails a logically dependent opposition.

The first claim explains why Primal One does not require a prior counterpart. The second expresses the author's adopted Shadow principle.

Simultaneity of obtaining does not imply equality of logical priority.

### 5. The Shadow relationship

The author adopts the Shadow relationship:

\[
\boxed{S(P)=-P}
\]

This is a defining rule of the proposed construction, not a theorem derived from distinction alone.

The negative notation represents opposition relative to the same primal magnitude. It does not yet presuppose the complete arithmetic structure of negative integers.

The Shadow preserves the magnitude of Primal One while expressing its opposed valence.

### 6. Why equal magnitude?

The author's proposed explanation is structural.

Primal One is one realized determination. Its Shadow expresses opposition within that same constitutive relationship.

The opposition does not independently generate another magnitude.

Consequently, the magnitude remains invariant under Shadow:

\[
|P|=|S(P)|
\]

The notation is illustrative. It expresses the intended invariance without claiming that conventional absolute value has already been constructed.

The distinction between magnitude and valence allows the author to propose two opposed orientations of a single foundational magnitude.

The existence of relational opposition alone does not prove equal magnitude. Equality is supplied by the adopted claim that Shadow preserves the magnitude of Primal One.

This makes the commitment explicit rather than hiding it in familiar notation.

### 7. An analogy from loss

The author's apple example illustrates the relational character of negative quantity.

If a person possesses one apple and consumes it, the resulting possession is zero apples, while the change is negative one apple.

The negative quantity describes a relationship to the original positive magnitude.

This does not mean that negative quantities always require a prior historical possession. Negative quantities may also be defined relative to a reference state.

The analogy clarifies the role of opposition but does not independently derive the Shadow principle.

## III. Primal Combination and Primal Zero

### 8. The first cancellation

Primal One and its Shadow are opposed determinations of the same magnitude.

The author adopts their cancellation through Primal Combination.

Using \(C\) to represent the foundational combination relationship:

\[
\boxed{C(P,S(P))=Z}
\]

Here \(Z\) designates Primal Zero.

This is an adopted constitutive rule, not a deduction from a previously established addition operation.

The familiar arithmetic expression

\[
1+(-1)=0
\]

is a later formal representation of the proposed cancellation.

It must not be used to explain the foundational event by assuming that ordinary addition and additive inverses already exist.

### 9. Primal Zero is determinate

Primal Zero is a fully designated result.

It is not the unnameable, and cancellation does not return the construction to its original condition.

The distinction is:

- The unnameable permits no distinguishable members.
- Primal One is the first realized determination.
- The Shadow is its logically dependent opposition.
- Primal Zero is the determinate result of their combination.

The terms *member* and *participant* must not be conflated.

Primal One and its Shadow are **participants** in cancellation. This does not make them members of zero in a set-theoretic sense.

### 10. Zero and the integers

The author describes zero as containing all integers through their cancellation relationships.

In established arithmetic:

\[
n+(-n)=0
\]

for every integer \(n\).

This is a useful way to interpret the author's claim: every integer participates in a relationship yielding zero.

It does not mean that zero is a set containing all integers, or that an infinite sum of every integer has been evaluated.

The general integer relationship belongs to established mathematics. The foundational investigation proposes its first constitutive precursor.

The emergence of Primal Zero does not by itself prove the existence of the complete integer system.

That proof is not the objective of this investigation.

## IV. What the Construction Does and Does Not Establish

### 11. The proposed foundational structure

The relationships may be summarized as follows:

\[
\begin{gathered}

\end{gathered}
\]

The arrows indicate logical or constitutive dependence, not temporal succession.

The encompassing bounding is logically dependent upon primal bounding.

Primal One and Shadow obtain simultaneously, though Shadow is logically secondary.

The cancellation relationship is adopted.

The diagram is a compact representation of the author's proposal, not an independent proof of its necessity.

### 12. Adopted principles

The present construction adopts:

**Primal Shadow**

\[
S(P)=-P
\]

**Primal cancellation**

\[
C(P,S(P))=Z
\]

For subsequent formal combination, the author also adopts associativity and commutativity.

These laws are compatible with the intended arithmetic development but have not been derived from primal bounding.

Making them explicit prevents the construction from claiming more than it has established.

### 13. The emergence of additional numerical units

Primal One is the first realized determination.

A second arithmetic unit cannot be obtained merely by counting the bounding relationships or by treating unnamed possibilities as already realized individuals.

A construction of two requires an additional realized determination and a premise establishing its apartness from the first.

Only then can an expression such as

\[
1+1=2
\]

be interpreted as combining distinct numerical units.

This remains a transition requiring explanation if the investigation is extended beyond Primal Zero.

The author has not committed to whether primal cancellation must be the first instance of combination in every relevant sense, or whether another realized determination may enter a combination first.

That ordering question remains open.

## V. Contact, Power, and Compatibility

### 14. Primal Power as a precursor

The author proposes that nested bounding, identified as Primal Power, anticipates numerical power and contact.

This is a foundational interpretation, not an assertion that ordinary exponentiation and multiplication are identical operations.

The proposal is that both may express relationships originating in nested constitution.

The precise mathematical laws of contact do not follow merely from naming this precursor.

They may be adopted and examined for consistency with the foundational structure.

### 15. Distributivity

In established arithmetic, contact and combination satisfy:

\[
a(b+c)=ab+ac
\]

The author's position is that distributivity is an **adopted compatibility consistent with the foundational construction**.

It is not derived from Primal Power, valence, or combination.

The fact that an encompassing bounding leaves the primal bounding unchanged provides a possible heuristic for investigating structural preservation.

But preserving an inner bounding is not the same proposition as distributing contact over combination.

The former does not establish the latter.

### 16. A coherence check

Once ordinary arithmetic operations and their familiar properties have been adopted, we can examine cancellation under contact:

\[
a(1+(-1))=a0=0
\]

and

\[
a(1)+a(-1)=a+(-a)=0
\]

The results agree.

This is a **coherence check**, not a derivation of distributivity.

It shows that the adopted relationships can operate together without conflict in this instance.

The compatibility can be mutually supporting without being a viciously circular proof, provided the adopted premises are not misrepresented as independently derived conclusions.

No foundational proof of distributivity is required for the present investigation to reach its intended endpoint.

## VI. Comparisons with Established Mathematics

### 17. The empty set

In conventional set theory, the empty set is a determinate object:

\[
\varnothing
\]

It has no members, but it can participate in other mathematical relationships.

For example:

\[
\varnothing\in\{\varnothing\}
\]

The set on the right has one member: the empty set.

This demonstrates that a memberless object can participate in a larger structure without acquiring members itself.

The comparison is suggestive but not an identification.

The unnameable is not a set. The primal bounding is nameable, while what it bounds remains without distinguishable members.

The encompassing bounding takes in the primal bounding, not the contents of the unnameable.

### 18. Nested structures

Nested sets provide another comparison:

\[
\varnothing,\quad \{\varnothing\},\quad
\{\{\varnothing\}\}
\]

Each expression is a determinate mathematical object.

Nested bounding in the foundational proposal is different because it concerns the constitution of determination rather than operations on already defined sets.

Nevertheless, both exhibit the possibility of a new relational structure without populating the original interior.

The analogy may help explain the proposal. It does not prove it.

### 19. The example of \(0^0\)

In finite combinatorics, \(b^a\) counts the functions from an \(a\)-element set to a \(b\)-element set.

There is exactly one function from the empty set to itself: the empty function.

Consequently, in that context:

\[
0^0=1
\]

This demonstrates that a determinate relationship involving empty domains can have a unique realization.

It does not establish that Primal Power is ordinary exponentiation.

Its relevance is conceptual: an absence of members need not imply an absence of relational structure.

The author has identified this mathematical phenomenon as one source of inspiration for the investigation.

## VII. Assessment and Open Questions

### 20. What the reconstruction accomplishes

The investigation proposes a coherent route from primal distinction to a first realized determination, its logically dependent Shadow, and a determinate result of cancellation.

It distinguishes the unnameable from mathematical zero.

It separates foundational constitution from the later arithmetic notation used to represent it.

And it makes clear that adopted rules need not be disguised as derivations.

These are substantive clarifications.

They do not establish that primal bounding is the unique possible foundation of numerical structure.

### 21. What remains open

Several questions remain unresolved:

**The scope of encompassing bounding.** What does the second bounding bound the whole against, given that the first already establishes the limit of the nameable against the unnameable?

**The transition to further quantities.** What constitutes an additional realized determination, and what establishes its apartness?

**The order of combination.** Must primal cancellation be the first instance of combination, or can other realized determinations be combined first?

**The necessity of the foundational rules.** Can Shadow and cancellation be shown to follow necessarily from the bounding relationship, rather than being adopted as compatible principles?

Other questions—including the source of potentiality, the status of the unnameable as relative or absolute, and the terminology of Primal Power—remain reserved for the human author.

None must be resolved to state the present proposal accurately.
